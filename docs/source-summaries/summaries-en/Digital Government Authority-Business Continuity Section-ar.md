# DGA Business Continuity Management System: supplied Arabic extract

## Identity and relationship to the complete guide
Digital Government Authority (Saudi Arabia), Arabic Guideline for Risk Management and Business Continuity for Digital Government, document DGA-1-2-5-231, 13 January 2025. Citation key: dga2025bcm. The complete Arabic guide's cover identifies version 3.0 despite its V1.0 filename. This 54-page supplied extract contains the BCM divider, printed pp. 98–130, an unnumbered management-review page, documentation/definitions and annex material through printed p. 149, then a closing page. The BCM division starts at printed p. 97 in the complete guide. The earlier bibliography's pp. 98–146 did not describe the full extract accurately.

All extract pages and relevant figures/tables were examined. This is a primary guidance document, not an empirical study. The pre-existing PDF is preserved unchanged; its public classification should not be interpreted as a newly verified open redistribution licence. The full guide and extract represent one work, not independent corroboration.

## Governance and process
Sections 5.2.1–5.2.4 discuss continuity of digital-government services, decision support, leadership, organizational context, policy, resources, internal/external dependencies, training and improvement. Automation and regular reporting are success factors. Governance distinguishes board/steering oversight, continuity officials/champions, ICT, cybersecurity, HR, facilities, suppliers, auditors and unit managers. Role diagrams and responsibilities are indicative and must fit the entity (pp. 98–114).

Section 5.2.5 organizes work into Plan–Do–Check–Act. Planning sets scope, policy, objectives, roles and awareness. Activation includes BIA, risk/threat assessment, strategies, continuity/ICT recovery plans and incident-response structure. Verification uses exercises/tests; correction includes performance review, audit and improvement. This is a BCMS lifecycle rather than an automatic priority-calculation specification.

## BIA, temporal impacts and platform classification
Section 5.2.5.2.1 (pp. 115–119) inventories operations/procedures and dependencies, evaluates disruption consequences over time using the entity's approved impact matrix, identifies critical activities and records recovery requirements. Outputs include RTO, RPO, MTPD, MBCO and required people, equipment, systems and facilities. The assessment timeline depends on service sensitivity: half-hour intervals and day-scale starting points are contextual examples (p. 116).

Figure 59 uses stakeholder, financial, legal/regulatory and reputational impact to classify government platforms/applications. Where factors differ, the highest impact level is used. Its five levels map to RTO examples of 4, 8, 12, 24 and 48 hours (very critical, critical, important, moderate, low); targets remain subject to review (pp. 117–118). Table 24 illustrates two platforms both classified critical with an eight-hour RTO, despite different lower category ratings.

These documented rules are prior art for maximum-impact classification and tier-to-RTO mapping. They should not be presented as a new invention of TDRP. Equally, the figure is not a complete temporal-impact derivation algorithm: the project must explain its own time-band interpretation, configuration and exceptions. Do not automatically adopt every financial cut-off, especially the poorly rendered low-tier percentage, without checking the original figure.

## Recovery, evaluation and information management
BCP and ICT DR plans should follow business requirements; capability gaps feed strategy (pp. 120–124). Plans distinguish crisis management, emergency response, communications, logistical support and assessment. Exercises include discussion/tabletop, procedural and full-scope approaches; reports document objectives, participants, scenarios, actual versus target times and lessons (pp. 125–127). Indicators, independent audit, corrective measures and management review maintain the system (pp. 128–131).

Documentation management (p. 132) calls for standardized BIA/risk/test forms, protected archiving and workflow/data/report automation. Definitions distinguish RTO, RPO, MTPD and MBCO (p. 137). Annex 9.3 (p. 147) identifies missing system priorities, missing dependency maps, unclear responsibility, untested plans and inadequate restart procedures as common planning mistakes. Other annexes provide risk examples, professional credentials, ICT incident-recovery phases and response structures; they are illustrative, not outcome data.

## Limits and use
Primary §2.1.2 Saudi guidance; major support for §§2.2–2.5 and synthesis §2.7. No sample, effect size or validation of Masar is reported. It supports traceable service/platform impacts, contextual criteria, dependencies and human approval. Implementing some supported functions does not establish full DGA compliance. The guide cites older ISO editions in places; use the separately verified current catalogue metadata when identifying ISO publications, without silently rewriting what this 2025 document cited.
