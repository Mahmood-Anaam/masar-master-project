# DGA integrated risk and business continuity guideline: complete Arabic file

## Identity and precise review scope
Digital Government Authority, Arabic integrated guideline, DGA-1-2-5-231, **version 3.0**, 13 January 2025. Key: dga2025bcm. The user-supplied filename contains V1.0, but the cover controls identification. This is the 150-page parent document of the separately supplied 54-page BCM extract. Both use one bibliography entry and must not be counted as independent studies.

Review coverage is deliberately explicit: the cover, contents, introduction/objectives/scope, BCM division (§5.2, pp. 97–131), documentation (§6), definitions/abbreviations and BCM-relevant annexes were examined. The long risk-management division (§5.1, pp. 6–96) is contextual material, **not a fully synthesized risk-methods corpus**; no detailed claim from its unreviewed procedures is licensed by this summary. For the detailed BCM process, time-parameter, classification, governance and evaluation extraction, use the companion summary “Digital Government Authority-Business Continuity Section-ar.md” in this same directory. That companion is an integral part of this parent-source summary.

## Whole-document purpose and structure
The introduction explains that the Arabic document integrates and updates earlier risk-management and business-continuity guides. It aims to support government entities' implementation of DGA's controls, strengthen reliable digital services, coordinate entities and improve practices and maturity. It treats application as dependent on operational facts, stakeholder context and relevant standards/regulatory requirements, not a universal identical implementation (pp. 3–5).

The contents separate risk management, BCM, documentation, definitions, abbreviations and annexes. This arrangement distinguishes an enterprise risk system from continuity analysis while connecting them through governance and shared information. The guide's benefits and strategic rationale are institutional guidance; they are not experimental estimates of improved recovery.

## BCM evidence carried by this parent document
Section 5.2 requires understanding services, operations and internal/external dependencies; impact analysis over entity-specific time intervals; critical activities; RTO, RPO, MTPD, MBCO and resources; followed by strategies and plans, exercises, audit and improvement. Figure 59 takes the highest impact among stakeholder, financial, legal/regulatory and reputation factors and maps five platform levels to 4/8/12/24/48-hour recovery targets subject to review. Table 24 illustrates its application. These substantive details and cautions are fully developed in the companion summary, with printed-page locators.

Section 6 (p. 132) links protected archiving, standardized forms and automation to consistent information and governance reporting. Annex 9.3 (p. 147) explicitly identifies absent system priorities, missing dependency mapping, unclear coordination, insufficient testing and missing restart instructions as planning mistakes. Annexes also illustrate resource-related risk categories and response structures; no empirical system-comparison experiment is reported.

Existing PDF bytes are preserved. A public-classification label does not itself establish an open redistribution licence. Attribution and the original filename are maintained; the source manifest ties this file and its extract to the same work.

## Use in the approved chapter
Primary §2.1 Saudi guidance; secondary §§2.2–2.5 and §2.7. Cite dga2025bcm once for a supported proposition and locate it in the relevant version. The guide supports the need for organized and traceable continuity information. It also supplies prior classification/rule examples, so the project's contribution must lie in its specific temporal method, configuration, implementation and evaluation. A BIA support system implements a subset of BCMS work; it is not proof of full compliance, governance completion or operational recovery capability.
