# Chapter 2: search and selection record

Search and reading date: 16 October 2026. Repository: `Mahmood-Anaam/masar-master-project`, branch `develop`.

## Review purpose

Establish the conceptual, empirical and methodological basis for Masar, particularly the relationship between time-indexed impact assessments, qualified recovery requirements, dependency information and traceable relative priorities. This is a structured thematic review for a non-thesis IS595 project, not a claim to a comprehensive systematic review or meta-analysis.

Public scholarly web searches, publisher pages, institutional repositories, Crossref DOI metadata and OpenAlex open-access locations were used. No authenticated Scopus/Web of Science search was performed. Searches were iterative and included DOI/title searches and follow-up references from included papers. The query families below describe the actual search focus, rather than asserting a fixed Boolean database export or an exhaustive search count:

- Business continuity management; frameworks; business continuity information systems.
- Business impact analysis; recovery time objective; critical functions and resources.
- Recovery prioritization; dependencies; decision support; restoration scheduling.
- Process mining; cybersecurity impact propagation across business processes.
- Business continuity maturity; self-assessment; FAMMOCN; evaluation.
- Design science artifact evaluation; FEDS.

The coverage includes older distinct approaches and recent publications through the search date. No universal publication-start-date filter was applied. References to standards in reviewed articles are attributed to those articles; inaccessible ISO full texts are not claimed to have been independently read.

## Selection and reading criteria

Substantive inclusion required relevance to the review questions, a verifiable DOI and an accessible full text. Selection sought complementary approaches, rather than a fixed number of papers per year. Fourteen academic articles were read completely before chapter synthesis. Two NIST guidance documents clarify terminology and requirements; their status is normative guidance, not experimental validation. NIST IR 8286D-upd1 was read completely; SP 800-34 Rev.1 was read selectively in the sections listed in its note.

A tracked working candidate set comprised 27 records: 16 included and 11 not included in this chapter's full-text comparison. These are working selection counts, **not** PRISMA search-result totals. Search engines did not provide a stable deduplicated export suitable for such a flow diagram. Other references already present in the repository's bibliography are not automatically part of this tracked set.

## Extraction and synthesis

Each note records design, context, inputs, outputs, evidence, limitations, relevant page locations and implications for Masar. The comparison distinguishes survey association, illustrative calculation, simulated tool use, actual organizational logs, computational experiments and methodological guidance. It does not pool heterogeneous outcomes.

Two safeguards affected synthesis: the Romanian studies share sample characteristics and dates, so independence is not assumed; the two FAMMOCN articles belong to the same research programme, so they are not counted as independent validation across twenty organizations. Expected reductions, agreement percentages, association confidence, maturity scores and actual recovery outcomes are explicitly separated.

Full-text availability, English-language coverage, purposive selection and absence of a Saudi organizational case limit completeness and transferability. The gap is bounded to the reviewed evidence; no claim is made that no commercial or unpublished system provides any individual feature.
