# Critical reading: paunescu2018bia

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2478/mmcks-2018-0021

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/paunescu2018bia.pdf) — 16 PDF pages.

Reading: complete academic article, 16 October 2026.

Complete article, tables and references read. Structured face-to-face survey in Bucharest, October 2017–January 2018: 146 questionnaires collected, 27 excluded, 119 SMEs analysed (pp.7–8). Regression links a composite BIA measure with resource-identification practices.

Eight predictors retained after correlation-based selection. Model R-squared .405; five significant adjusted associations are suppliers/partners, expertise, alternative premises, communication and information. IT, locations and plant are not individually significant in the final model (pp.9–12). Overall model significance does not imply that every predictor is significant, despite imprecise wording around ANOVA.

The dependent BIA construct itself incorporates critical resources, creating conceptual overlap with its predictors. One respondent per firm, local sample and self-reports constrain generalization; 14% had other decision-making roles (p.14). Outcomes are not measured interruption losses or recovery times. Nonsignificant adjusted IT coefficient is not evidence that IT is unnecessary.

Masar implication: resource model must include people, facilities, communication, information and external suppliers, rather than applications alone. Scope each dependency and its owner. The 2020 paper shares the same sample size, collection period and respondent profile; treat the two as related evidence, not independent replications.
