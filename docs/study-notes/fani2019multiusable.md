# Fani and Subriadi (2019): Multi-usable BCP framework

- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2019.11.124
- **Full text:** `../studies/fani2019multiusable.pdf`, 8 pages; complete reading, 16 October 2026.
- **Design/evidence:** Comparison of framework application in banking, electricity, water services, and manufacturing; Sections 3–6, pp. 278–282; Tables/Figures 1–2.

## Findings and critique

The study examines an eight-element, 38-activity planning framework across four organizational cases. Reported adaptations include putting impact analysis before prioritization and adding budgeting. Some training, testing, and periodic review activities were not performed because of organizational policy or time constraints. The final modified framework is proposed for subsequent testing.

The article supports contextual adaptation, not universal effectiveness. Its concluding limitation refers to the revised framework's untested status, while earlier sections describe application of the predecessor framework. Those two stages must remain distinct. Completing a planning document is not evidence of operational recovery capability. Missing exercise activities also weaken any claim that the resulting plans were validated under disruption.

## Implication for Masar

Keep impact assessment upstream of priority derivation. Distinguish configuration/adaptation from empirical validation. Document who supplies and approves recovery information and ensure that a completed software record is not reported as evidence of a tested continuity arrangement.
