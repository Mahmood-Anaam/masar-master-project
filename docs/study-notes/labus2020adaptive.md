# Critical reading: labus2020adaptive

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2298/CSIS190202037L

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/labus2020adaptive.pdf) — 28 PDF pages.

Reading: complete academic article, 16 October 2026.

Full article including questionnaire appendix read. The first author is Milica Labus, not Aleksandra. Framework evaluated in three Serbian financial institutions with 38 key users: 3, 9 and 26 respectively (pp.7–11). Four components cover BIA, risk assessment, BCP and continual improvement.

BIA and continual improvement have significant direct associations with adaptive e-BCM in the reported PLS-SEM model; the direct BIA-to-BCP hypothesis is unsupported. Authors subsequently propose an additional recovery-objectives parameter (pp.17–19). The revised parameter is a design response, not separately validated evidence. Questionnaire mean for continual improvement is 3.36, versus 4.58 for BIA (pp.12–13, appendix).

Small pooled sample, same sector and country, self-reported effectiveness and overlap between risk and planning constructs constrain inference. Nine indicators were removed in measurement refinement; one Fornell–Larcker discrepancy was accepted by authors (pp.14–15). Cross-sectional structural paths do not establish intervention causality, and PLS-SEM does not remove small-sample uncertainty. Table 6 and prose differ on one insignificant coefficient; do not repeat an unqualified precise coefficient for that path.

Masar implication: explicitly connect assessed recovery requirements with planning records and review triggers. Define approval and revision states; do not treat possession of a BCP as demonstrated recoverability. Not a tested web application comparison.
