# Critical reading: russo2022maturity

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e10566

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/russo2022maturity.pdf) — 15 PDF pages.

Reading date: 16 October 2026.

Full article, figures, tables and references read. DSR framework FAMMOCN comprises model, measurement system, self-assessment system and implementation guide. Eight BCM components support progressive adoption and weighted metrics with owner, source of evidence, frequency and review attributes (pp.6–9).

Five experts participated in a two-hour focus group. Ten medium/large organizations subsequently participated through advance self-assessment and simulated metric completion during semi-structured interviews (pp.10–12). Interviews were not recorded to protect confidentiality. This substituted for a longitudinal organizational case study (p.11). Positive agreement percentages concern perceived attributes; they are not recovery success rates. Microenterprise transfer is limited because smaller organizations were difficult to recruit.

Visibility of weights was retained to improve transparency, while experts warned that too many metrics and excessive parameterization increase workload (pp.11–13). Measurement maturity and documentation coverage do not establish the feasibility of any particular RTO. Software tools support the framework, but online integration was future work (p.14).

Masar implication: preserve evidence and ownership, avoid hiding weak service-level recovery evidence behind an overall maturity average, and evaluate whether information links help specific tasks. This research and Russo et al. (2025) belong to the same research programme, so their evaluations cannot automatically be counted as independent replications.
