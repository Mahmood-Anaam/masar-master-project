# Critical reading: swanson2010contingency

DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-34r1

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/swanson2010contingency.pdf) — 149 PDF pages.

Reading date: 16 October 2026.

Targeted normative reading, not full-report reading: PDF pp.8–10 (contents), 27–33 (printed13–19: planning process, BIA and resource priorities), 41–46 (printed27–32: testing, training, exercises and maintenance), 119–124 (Appendix B in full, plus first two FAQ pages). The full 149-page report is supplied for reference; unrelated technology-specific appendices were not used as evidence.

Section 3.2 distinguishes process MTD, system-resource RTO and data RPO, and explains why system RTO normally leaves time for process resumption before MTD. An infeasible target requires a documented mitigation plan; specifying a target does not establish capability. Resource dependencies and recovery priority hierarchy are explicit.

Appendix B (PDF119–122) includes critical processes, impact categories, timing objectives and their drivers, alternative means, resource lists and recovery order. Its sample times are illustrative and not reusable organizational defaults. Sections 3.5–3.6 emphasize exercises, controlled distribution, change records and plan currency.

Masar implication: separate business-service tolerance, resource restoration target and recovery evidence; retain ownership/version/approval information and distinguish test evidence from proposed requirements. US federal compliance statements are outside Masar’s asserted obligations. This guidance is not an empirical comparison study.
