# Critical reading: shahverdi2025recovery

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00291-024-00777-9

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/shahverdi2025recovery.pdf) — 37 PDF pages.

Reading: complete academic article, 16 October 2026.

Stochastic integer-programming decision support for restoring interdependent road, water and power networks, prioritizing hospital functionality. Rolling decisions incorporate observations and permit decision-maker overrides. Read the complete article, appendix and references. pp. 1–20 develop the model and algorithm; pp. 21–31 report computational experiments; pp. 32–35 explain limitations and worked example.

The illustrative regional network has 182 lifeline elements and two hospitals. A prioritization comparison restores hospitals in 11 rather than 13 time increments while overall completion remains 57 in that case. This is conditional computational evidence, not a field experiment or a universal gain. Scenario reduction and parallelization improve solvability under the reported computational conditions.

The model assumes binary functionality, known repair durations of one time increment, negligible travel time relative to repair, and immediate hospital functionality once its lifelines are restored. Longer, uncertain repairs and partial restoration require extensions (p.32). The appendix’s network-state tables are graphical; their structure was inspected as part of the worked example, without transcribing their entries into Masar.

Masar implication: distinguish business urgency, prerequisite relations and a feasible resource-constrained schedule. Masar will expose prerequisites and conflicts; it does not reproduce this scheduling optimization. Recovery weights and resource assumptions must remain visible. Publication is in the 2025 issue; online publication was in 2024.
