# Arias-Aranda et al. (2026): BCM process reference model

- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/app16073219
- **Full text:** `../studies/arias2026processes.pdf`, 21 pages; complete reading, 16 October 2026.
- **Design:** Standards mapping, expert questionnaire/interviews, and an initial public-sector pilot.
- **Evidence:** Methods, pp. 6–9; expert participation and results, pp. 10–13; process model, pp. 14–16; limitations/future work, pp. 17–19.

## Findings and critique

The authors propose 22 BCM processes differentiated into management, core, and support processes. Thirty-nine of 47 German specialists responded; an 80% threshold informed consensus categories. The model relates processes to inputs, outputs, responsibilities, and management cycles. The pilot indicates that organizational adaptation is necessary.

Expert agreement supports content relevance, not measured software effectiveness or causal resilience improvement. Several processes below the consensus threshold were retained after standards-based argument; governance and some support processes also received high recognition despite the final category distinctions. The resulting model is therefore a synthesis of expert judgments and author interpretation, not a purely mechanical consensus result. The German-centered panel and limited pilot constrain transferability. Detailed flowcharts and a fuzzy decision support system remain future work.

## Implication for Masar

Use process inputs, outputs, responsibilities, and review states to structure continuity information. A bounded Masar scope need not implement all 22 processes. Distinguish the authors' standards interpretation from direct access to the standards; this study does not establish ISO certification or supply a validated TDRP rule set.
