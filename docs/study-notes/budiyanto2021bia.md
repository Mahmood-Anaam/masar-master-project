# Budiyanto, Gurning and Pitana (2021): Electricity disruption at a container terminal

- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su132112038
- **Full text:** `../studies/budiyanto2021bia.pdf`, 17 pages; complete reading, 16 October 2026.
- **Design/context:** Single container-terminal case in Surabaya; 30 management respondents, agreed impact scales, and operational performance calculations.
- **Evidence:** Sections 3–4, PDF pp. 4–14; impact scales, Table 3; calculations, Tables 4–7; classification, Table 8; discussion, pp. 14–15.

## Findings and critique

Six impact dimensions are considered: terminal productivity, economic loss, employee productivity, customers, reputation, and wasted energy. The study combines management preferences with historical parameters and an 18-hour electricity interruption. It illustrates how consequences and their evaluation depend on operational context.

The calculated values are case-specific rather than transferable recovery targets. Several results use proxies, such as container throughput for revenue and ship calls for customers. There is also a classification inconsistency: Table 3 places 93.78% remaining income in the major interval (92–94%), whereas Table 8 labels that result catastrophic. This warrants independent checking before adapting a matrix. The paper does not validate an automated service-prioritization system or establish general MTPD/RTO conversion rules.

## Implication for Masar

Store the quantitative evidence separately from its qualitative classification and approved threshold. Define boundary inclusivity and the difference between percentage remaining and percentage lost. Use synthetic cases to verify the consistency of matrix labels, ranges, and calculated outputs. Preserve context and provenance instead of copying the terminal's thresholds.
