# Critical reading: russo2025self

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/technologies13060242

Local full text: [PDF](../studies/russo2025self.pdf) — 38 PDF pages.

Reading date: 16 October 2026.

Complete article, figures, tables, limitations and references read. Builds on FAMMOCN with eight components, 118 activities and 61 selected self-assessment questions. Hierarchical weights produce 0–100 scores; indicators are red below 60, yellow from 60 to below 90, green from 90 (pp.18–27). Figures use randomly assigned demonstration values (p.23), not measured organizational results.

Five-expert focus group and interviews with ten organizations (30% medium, 70% large) support perceived usefulness and iterative refinement (pp.27–31). The delivery-sector case expects a 50% reduction in recovery time; this is an expectation, not an observed recovery result (pp.30–31). Mobile/web application and fuller longitudinal studies remain future work (pp.33–34). Alignment with other frameworks requires further testing.

Reporting cautions: a 70% participant agreement in a five-person focus group cannot be an unweighted whole-person fraction without an additional denominator (p.29). Section 7.4 mentions six-month score progression whereas conclusions say full longitudinal case studies were not conducted. Treat these claims cautiously rather than interpreting them as controlled longitudinal evidence. Some standard/model descriptions and selected activity totals are not fully reconciled. Hierarchical percentages need normalization; do not copy a displayed multiplication formula without checking units.

Masar implication: retain question/assessment justification and make configuration visible, but distinguish organizational maturity score, service impact severity and recovery priority. No generic semaphore cutoffs will be imported into TDRP. Because this is a continuation of the 2022 framework, do not claim independent validation across twenty organizations.
