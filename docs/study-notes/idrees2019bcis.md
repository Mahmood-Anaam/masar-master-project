## Findings and critical interpretation

The framework connects survey preparation and statistical analysis to association discovery and BCM activities. The paper reports 93 selected rules with average confidence of 94%, after filtering rules generated from a transformed dataset. Its BIA diagram includes dependencies and recovery parameters. The case identifies awareness, training, and information-management concerns.

Association-rule confidence is a conditional frequency in the analyzed data, not recovery accuracy, causal evidence, or independent validation. The transformation from questionnaires to 17,683 records is insufficiently transparent for treating those records as independent participants. Some statistical reporting is inconsistent: the stated confidence/Z pairing and the prose interpretation of explained variance warrant caution. The work presents a framework and case analysis, not a controlled demonstration of operational downtime reduction or a validated end-to-end prioritization engine.

## Implication for Masar

Distinguish empirically mined associations from organization-approved decision rules. Reuse the concern for structured data and stakeholder context, while explicitly documenting TDRP inputs, threshold semantics, approval, and expected outputs. The paper already discusses BIA parameters and dependencies, so neither feature can support an absolute novelty claim for Masar.
