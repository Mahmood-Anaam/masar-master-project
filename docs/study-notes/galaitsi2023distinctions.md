# Galaitsi et al. (2023): Continuity and resilience concepts

- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s13753-023-00494-x
- **Local full text:** `../studies/galaitsi2023distinctions.pdf` (9 pages).
- **Reading:** Complete article, including conceptual framework, illustrative cases, discussion, and references; 16 October 2026.
- **Design:** Conceptual synthesis, not an intervention trial or validation of a software artifact.
- **Evidence locations:** Sections 1–2, pp. 713–716; Table 1 and illustrative cases, pp. 717–719; recommendations/conclusion, pp. 719–720.

## Findings and critical interpretation

The authors distinguish BCM, operational resilience, and organizational resilience using three dyads: processes/assets, risk/resilience, and normal/crisis conditions. Examples include KBR, Martha Stewart Living, airlines, and LEGO. These illustrate the proposed interpretation; they are not controlled tests of the distinctions. The paper recommends identifying critical processes and assets and examining potential cascades between them.

Its value for Masar is conceptual discipline: a continuity information system should specify what it supports and which outcomes it evaluates. The dyadic definitions are the authors' proposal, not universally settled terminology. The crisis boundary is contextual and partly subjective, limiting its direct use as a numerical threshold. Illustrative cases rely on published accounts and do not establish that applying the framework improves continuity performance.

## Implication for Masar

Maintain separate service/process and supporting-resource entities and document dependencies. Evaluate traceability and task support; do not equate those results with demonstrated organizational resilience. No TDRP rule, MTPD calculation, or software architecture is validated by this article.
