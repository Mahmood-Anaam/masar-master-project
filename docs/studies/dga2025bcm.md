## Detailed summary

Describes a BCMS for government entities built on Plan-Do-Check-Act, aligned with ISO 22301:2019 and companion standards (ISO 22330:2018, ISO 22317:2015 on BIA, ISO 22398:2013) and with the National Framework (p. 102). Five benefits of the system are listed (Figure 52). The BIA (5.2.5.2.1, pp. 115-119) analyzes impact of interruption over time with an entity-approved matrix; the starting point of the time sequence depends on service sensitivity (30 minutes for some entities, a day or two for others; p. 116). Figure 58 shows RTO, RPO and MTPD on a timeline.

## Further details recorded

Figure 59 is a classification matrix for government digital platforms and applications with four impact factors (reputational, legal and regulatory, financial, beneficiary) and target recovery times: very critical 4 hours; critical 8 hours; important 12 hours; medium 24 hours; the highest level is taken where factors disagree (pp. 117-119). Financial thresholds read: 1,000,000 / 500,000 / 100,000 / 50,000 SAR per hour for the four levels. Section 5.2.5.2.4 (p. 121): IT and communications disaster recovery plans focus on the technical systems supporting critical procedures and must be aligned with business recovery requirements; gaps go into the continuity strategy. Section 5.2.5.2.3: continuity plans document internal and external dependencies. Figure 60 (p. 120) groups risks by resource type (documents, service providers, information systems, people, facilities).

## Relevance to the project

Provides the Saudi digital-government setting for the project and the clearest example of a time-based classification with target recovery times, plus the 'highest level' rule for conflicting factors.

## Limitations and cautions

The matrix is a classification device with fixed levels; it does not specify a procedure for deriving MTPD/RTO from graded impacts, nor criticality of supporting technical systems.
