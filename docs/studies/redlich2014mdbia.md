# Redlich et al. (2014)

**Citation key:** `redlich2014mdbia`  
**Type:** Conference paper  
**DOI / identifier:** 10.1145/2568088.2576797

## Bibliographic data

Redlich, D., Winkler, U., Molka, T., Gilani, W. (2014). Model-driven engineering in practice: Integrated performance decision support for process-centric business impact analysis. Proceedings of ICPE '14, Dublin, 247-258.

## Access level (what was actually read)

Full text read.

## Detailed summary

Observes that business processes and the resource infrastructure supporting them are treated as separate domains, so analysts must consolidate information by hand. Presents the Model-Driven Business Impact Analysis (MDBIA) workbench, extending a performance engineering workbench with a Generic Business Continuity Model (GenericBCM) metamodel (resources, dependencies with AND/OR groups, sporadic threats, failure effects, recovery) and a Performance Analysis Model, to predict the effect of disruptions on process performance by simulation.

## Further details recorded

Evaluation: qualitative analysis of an example with five resources (technical staff, desktop PCs, power, UPS, office) and a four-activity BPMN process, simulated over one year; flood and power disruption effects discussed; results judged reasonable. The tool is used in industrial use cases of the EU project TIMBUS (dam safety, eHealth). The authors state no modelling tool for failure dependencies existed and list future work: distributions for parameters, importing dependencies from landscape models, a general-purpose intermediate language, faster analysis, and real-time monitoring for continuous BIA.

## Relevance to the project

Closest academic model of dependencies between resources for BIA; contrasts with TDRP, which propagates criticality downward from services.

## Limitations and cautions

Design-time tool; qualitative evaluation on a small example; focuses on performance, not recovery prioritization.
