Horalek \cite{amm2023bia} & AMM datasets and systems; technical analysis and guided interviews; impact matrices inform tolerable periods and recovery objectives. & Availability assessed at seven durations. One domain-specific worked case; no controlled comparison of incident recovery outcomes. \\
Budiyanto et al. \cite{container2021bia} & Container-terminal electricity disruption; management judgments and operating records inform five-level impact classifications. & Observed disruption and subsequent consequences. Supports contextual impact assessment rather than a general MTPD or RTO derivation rule. \\
Sahebjamnia et al. \cite{sahebjamnia2015integrated} & BIA requirements, importance weights, resources, and budgets enter a multi-objective planning model; outputs include operating trajectories and allocations. & Discrete recovery periods. Manufacturing case and hypothetical disruption evaluation; tolerability requirements are inputs to planning. \\
Aghabegloo et al. \cite{assetcriticality2023} & Expert judgments, historical data, and risk scenarios; fuzzy decision methods and a Bayesian network produce asset criticality and contributor rankings. & Recovery-related quantities enter resilience assessment. Gas-plant case, expert face validation, and sensitivity analysis; no generic temporal service-threshold rule. \\
Von der Assen et al. \cite{vonderassen2024quanttm} & Threat probabilities and business-loss assumptions enter QuantTM; a browser prototype estimates expected financial loss. & Loss represented through recovery stages and time frames. Preprint and case-based evaluation; expected loss is not service recovery priority. \\
Raptaki et al. \cite{raptaki2025propagation} & Procurement event logs generate a weighted process graph; incident impact is propagated to rank activities. & Scenario-sensitive relative impact rather than tolerable-duration derivation. One organizational case with comparisons and interviews; coverage depends on logged activities. \\
