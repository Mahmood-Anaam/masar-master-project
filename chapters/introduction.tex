\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Organizations deliver services through interconnected applications, facilities, personnel, suppliers, and records. A disruption affecting one element may extend to dependent elements and impair multiple business services. Continuity teams must decide which services to restore first, how long each service can remain unavailable, and which supporting resources must be recovered before a service can resume. These decisions have legal, financial, and operational consequences and must often be made under time pressure and with incomplete information.

Business continuity management (BCM) prepares organizations to respond to disruptions and maintain prioritized services at acceptable levels. One of its central analytical activities is business impact analysis (BIA), which examines how the consequences of an interruption evolve over time and establishes recovery requirements such as the maximum tolerable period of disruption (MTPD) and the recovery time objective (RTO). These requirements are established before an incident occurs, yet they guide decisions during an incident. Therefore, the quality of recovery decisions depends on how these requirements are derived and whether the reasoning behind them can be reproduced, examined, and justified.

During a disruption, recovery decisions also require timely and accurate information about affected services, technical systems, dependencies, and recovery procedures. When this information is distributed across multiple documents and files, continuity teams may need to collect and review it manually, delaying decisions at a time when rapid action is essential.

To address these challenges, this project proposes Masar, a rule-based methodology supported by a decision support system. The methodology is the primary contribution of the project. It defines rules for deriving recovery requirements from time-dependent impact profiles and prioritizing recovery actions according to impact severity, time constraints, and dependencies between services and technical systems. The decision support system implements the proposed methodology, centralizes relevant continuity information, and presents recovery priorities through a dashboard. It is intended to support decision-making rather than replace human judgment.

This chapter presents the background of the project, defines the problem being addressed, and describes the proposed solution. It then outlines the project aim and objectives, significance, approach, scope, expected deliverables, assumptions and limitations, and the organization of the report.


\section{Background}

\subsection{Business Continuity and Recovery Requirements}

Business continuity management is a management discipline that enables organizations to prepare for disruptions, respond effectively, and continue delivering prioritized products and services at an acceptable level. ISO 22301 provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving a business continuity management system. Business impact analysis (BIA) is a fundamental component of this process, as it identifies the consequences of disruptions over time and supports the establishment of recovery requirements. NIST guidance also provides relevant principles for contingency planning and system recovery 6.

Several recovery-related concepts are particularly relevant to this project:


* Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption (MTPD): The maximum period during which an activity or service can remain disrupted before the resulting consequences become unacceptable.
* Recovery Time Objective (RTO): The target time within which a service, activity, or supporting resource should be restored following a disruption.
* Recovery Point Objective (RPO): The point in time to which data must be recovered following an incident, defining the maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.
* Minimum Business Continuity Objective (MBCO): The minimum acceptable level of products or services that an organization must maintain during a disruption.

For these requirements to support effective decisions, they must have clearly defined scopes, units of measurement, approval status, and validity. Their relationship to the affected service or resource must also be clear.

The impact of a disruption is not constant; it may increase as the interruption continues. A service that can tolerate a short interruption may become increasingly critical as time passes and its operational, financial, or regulatory consequences accumulate. Business impact analysis therefore considers the development of impacts over time through a time-dependent impact profile 6. This profile provides a basis for deriving recovery requirements and understanding how the urgency of restoring a service changes throughout a disruption.

\subsection{Information Systems for Business Continuity}

Information systems can support business continuity activities by organizing relevant information, assisting analysis, and supporting recovery planning and decision-making. Previous studies have investigated different approaches, including data mining techniques 3, recovery effort estimation 5, process mining 7, and optimization of restoration decisions 8.

Although these approaches address aspects of recovery planning and decision-making, an additional challenge remains: connecting recovery requirements established before an incident with the prioritization decisions required during an actual disruption, while ensuring that the information needed to make those decisions is readily accessible.

Masar addresses this challenge by combining a rule-based methodology with a decision support system. The methodology defines how recovery requirements are derived and how recovery actions are prioritized, while the system provides a centralized view of relevant continuity information and presents the resulting priorities through a dashboard. This approach aims to improve the consistency, traceability, and accessibility of recovery decisions while preserving human accountability for the final decisions.

















\section{Problem Statement}

During a business disruption, continuity teams need to determine the recovery requirements of affected services, understand their criticality and dependencies, and establish the appropriate recovery priorities. Although organizations may maintain business impact analysis (BIA) results, recovery plans, technical information, and supporting records, accessing and connecting this information during an incident can require considerable manual effort because it is distributed across multiple documents and files.

The manual process of locating, reviewing, and connecting relevant information can delay access to the information required to establish recovery priorities. In addition, determining appropriate recovery requirements requires a consistent approach for translating the time-dependent impact of a disruption into defined recovery objectives. Recovery priorities must also account for service criticality and dependencies between services and their supporting technical systems.

To address these challenges, this project proposes Masar, an integrated web-based business continuity system incorporating a rule-based methodology called Time-Dependent Recovery Prioritization (TDRP). The methodology uses a predefined impact matrix and decision rules to evaluate business impact across successive time frames, determine the Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption (MTPD) and corresponding Recovery Time Objective (RTO), and establish recovery priorities based on recovery requirements, service criticality, and dependencies.

Masar integrates the methodology, the processes for determining recovery requirements and service priorities, the relevant business continuity information, and an operational dashboard within a single system. This enables continuity teams to access the results and the information underlying them in one place, reducing the need to manually retrieve and connect information from multiple documents and files.

Accordingly, the problem addressed by this project is the need for an integrated business continuity system that applies a structured, rule-based methodology to determine recovery requirements and priorities from time-dependent impact information, service criticality, and dependencies, while providing a consolidated view of the resulting information.

\section{Proposed Solution}

The proposed solution is Masar, an integrated web-based business continuity system that incorporates a rule-based methodology called Time-Dependent Recovery Prioritization (TDRP). The system is designed to determine recovery requirements, establish service recovery priorities, organize relevant business continuity information, and present the resulting outputs through an operational dashboard. The primary contribution of the project is the design and implementation of the rule-based methodology within the system.

The TDRP methodology uses a predefined impact matrix and a set of decision rules to evaluate the impact of a disruption across successive time frames. Based on the impact levels identified during these time frames, the methodology determines the applicable Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption (MTPD) and the corresponding Recovery Time Objective (RTO). Under the proposed approach, the MTPD is assigned according to the time frame in which the impact first reaches the defined high-impact threshold, while the RTO is determined according to the rules associated with that time frame. The specific time frames, impact thresholds, and recovery rules will be defined as part of the methodology design.

After determining the recovery requirements, the system establishes recovery priorities by applying the defined rules to service criticality, recovery requirements, and dependencies between services and supporting technical systems. This process provides a consistent basis for determining the relative priority of services while accounting for the relationships between dependent services and the technical resources required for recovery. The detailed impact matrix, decision rules, and calculation procedures will be specified and documented during the methodology design and development phase.

Masar incorporates a structured repository of business continuity information, including services, business impact analysis results, time-dependent impact assessments, service criticality, recovery requirements, dependencies, technical resources, recovery procedures, and relevant supporting records. This information is used by the system’s rule-based processes to determine recovery requirements and priorities.

The operational dashboard is an integral component of Masar. It presents the results generated by the methodology, including recovery requirements, service priorities, service criticality, and dependencies between services and supporting technical systems. By integrating the methodology, information processing, and results presentation within one system, Masar reduces the need for continuity teams to manually retrieve and connect information from multiple documents and files. The dashboard presents the calculated results and associated information; the system does not generate recovery recommendations, simulate disruption scenarios, or make operational decisions.

A rule-based approach is selected because its decision logic can be explicitly defined, reviewed, and validated by business continuity professionals. Given the same input data and rule configuration, the methodology is designed to produce consistent results. This approach supports transparency, traceability, and practical implementation within the scope of the project. Masar therefore combines the proposed recovery methodology, its implementation, the relevant business continuity information, and the dashboard in a single integrated system, while leaving operational recovery decisions under the responsibility of authorized personnel.
